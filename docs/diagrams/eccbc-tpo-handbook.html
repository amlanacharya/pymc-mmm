Every judgment call behind the ECCBC trade-promo roadmap. Each card shows its position in the six-phase map on the right, the real options, why you'd pick one, and how it plays out for ECCBC Morocco specifically.

Phase 1 · Grain & Data

Deciding what you're modeling at

Which dims to model

dims=(...)
Plain brief
Set the model grain to territory, channel, medaille, and SKU, at a weekly interval. Check later if every cell has enough data to support it.
Initial grain (this build)
territorychannelmedailleSKUweekly
Why this grain: this is the full reporting grain ECCBC wants ROI at, so it's the right starting point for dims rather than something to build up to later. The tradeoff from starting here is real and worth watching, not a reason to avoid it: four pooled dims means many territory×channel×medaille×SKU cells, and some will be thin. That's what Phase 1's grain check (next card) is for — it doesn't mean drop a dim, it means know which cells to trust yet.
ECCBC example
dims=("territory","channel","medaille","sku"), one row per week per combination that actually exists in the data (don't manufacture zero-filled rows for combinations that never occurred). Expect the Casablanca/Modern-Trade/flagship-SKU cells to have the deepest history and the smallest rural/low-volume-SKU cells to need the most scrutiny in the grain check.
Phase 1 of 6

Which target variable

target_column
Plain brief
Fit the model on a physical quantity. Keep money-based and availability metrics as reporting layers, not as the fitting target.
VariableWhat it isUse as target?
unit_casesStandard trade unit — physical volume movedYes — primary recommendation
volume_hlHectoliters — physical volume in beverage-industry unitsYes — equivalent choice to cases, pick one and stay consistent
revenuecases × realized price per caseNo — derive after fitting, don't fit on it directly
weighted_distribution% of category volume sold through stores stocking the SKU (ACV-style)No — this is a control/diagnostic, not something a promo directly saturates
avg_selling_priceRealized price per case, net of the promo discountNo — this is usually a control or a separate price-elasticity model
Why: a saturation curve is meant to describe diminishing physical response to promo activity. Revenue folds in price movement, which has its own dynamics unrelated to the promo mechanic and can distort the fitted curve's shape. Distribution and price are real, useful variables — they belong as controls in Phase 3, not as the thing being predicted.
ECCBC example
Fit on weekly unit_cases per territory/channel/medaille/SKU cell. Compute revenue afterward as unit_cases × avg_selling_price for reporting, and bring weighted_distribution in as a control if it varies meaningfully across ECCBC's SKUs.
Phase 1 of 6

How to check the grain you picked

param_stability()
Plain brief
Fit at the full grain. Check each cell's parameter spread across cross-validation folds before you trust it.
Why: a cell with too few promo-active weeks can't identify its own curve — its posterior falls back toward the population prior and can look deceptively stable. A wide, unstable interval across folds is the actual tell that a territory/channel/medaille/SKU cell is too thin to report on its own yet.
ECCBC example
If a low-volume rural SKU in Traditional Trade shows a much wider posterior interval than a flagship SKU in Casablanca Modern Trade for the same mechanic, don't drop SKU from dims globally — flag that specific cell as low-confidence and report it pooled up one level (e.g. territory × channel × medaille) until more history accumulates.

Phase 2 · Calendar & Adstock

Getting the calendar and carryover right

What goes on the calendar table

add_events(df_events=...)
Plain brief
List fixed holidays with their dates. List Ramadan and Eid with their real Gregorian dates for each year, not one repeating rule.
Why: fixed civic holidays repeat on the same Gregorian date every year, so a smooth annual Fourier term already half-covers them. Ramadan and Eid al-Fitr/al-Adha sit on the Hijri calendar and drift roughly 10 to 12 days earlier every Gregorian year — no fixed-period term can track that, so their real dates must be supplied directly.
ECCBC example
Build a lookup of Ramadan start/end and Eid al-Fitr/al-Adha dates for every year in ECCBC's history plus the next planning year, sourced from a Hijri calendar converter, and feed those into add_events().
Phase 2 of 6

One Ramadan event or three

EventEffect + GaussianBasis
Plain brief
Model the stock-up week, Ramadan itself, and Eid as three separate events. Do not fold them into one wide window.
Why: consumption shifts direction three times around this period — a stock-up spike beforehand, altered daytime-versus-iftar demand during, and a celebration spike right after Eid. One wide Gaussian bump averages three different shapes into a blurred one.
ECCBC example
Three events per year: pre_ramadan_stockup, ramadan, eid_al_fitr — each with its own GaussianBasis width, since the stock-up bump is typically narrower than the month-long Ramadan effect.
Phase 2 of 6

Adstock choice per promo mechanic — worked in detail

NoAdstock() / GeometricAdstock()
Plain brief
Look at how each mechanic works. Give it carryover only if its real effect lasts past the promo week.
Step 1 — classify each of ECCBC's 10 mechanics by how it actually acts on a shopper
Mechanic archetypeHow it actsStarting adstockReasoning
Immediate price-offCheaper this week onlyNoAdstock()Pure purchase-timing shift, no memory component
Free goods / BOGOExtra volume for the same spendNoAdstock()Same as above, plus it directly encourages pantry-loading (see next card)
Multi-buy / bundleDiscount conditional on buying more than oneNoAdstock()Contemporaneous; the "more than one" condition doesn't create a delayed effect
In-store display / end-capExtra shelf visibility while it's upGeometricAdstock, tight priorVisibility can nudge a shopper who returns a few days later — a small, real carryover
Sampling / trial mechanicFree trial meant to build a habitGeometricAdstock, wider priorThis one is explicitly designed to have an effect after the promo ends — closer to awareness-building than a discount
Loyalty points multiplierPoints banked for later redemptionGeometricAdstock, wider priorThe reward is deliberately delayed, so some carryover is structurally expected
Step 2 — don't just assume the table, check it empirically
Fit each mechanic's adstock with a wide, uninformative prior first (not NoAdstock() yet). Read the posterior on alpha afterward:
\[ w_t = \alpha^{t}, \qquad \alpha \sim \mathrm{Beta}(1,3) \]
If the posterior for alpha collapses close to 0, the data itself is telling you that mechanic has no real carryover — switch it to NoAdstock() for the final spec, since a near-zero alpha and no adstock produce the same curve but the simpler model is easier to explain and faster to sample. If alpha posterior sits meaningfully above 0 with a tight interval, keep the estimated adstock — the classification table is a starting hypothesis, the posterior is the actual answer.
Step 3 — half-life is the number to report to the trade team, not alpha
\[ \alpha = 0.5^{1/h} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h = \frac{\ln 0.5}{\ln \alpha} \]
Alpha is a Bayesian prior parameter, not a number ECCBC's trade team will find meaningful. Half-life h in weeks ("this mechanic's effect fades to half strength after h weeks") is the same information stated in a way a promo planner can act on directly.
ECCBC example
Fit ECCBC's free-goods and immediate-discount mechanics with a wide adstock prior first. If the posterior half-life comes back under roughly 1 week for both, lock them to NoAdstock() in the final spec and put the modeling effort instead into the lagged pantry-loading control (next card) — that is very likely where the real multi-week story is for these mechanic types, not in a positive carryover tail.
Phase 2 of 6

Add a lagged pantry-loading control?

control_columns
Plain brief
Add a control for last week's promo activity. Let its coefficient come out negative if the data supports that.
Why: a discount often borrows next week's sales forward — buyers stock up, then don't repurchase. Adstock cannot represent that negative give-back; a plain control can, since control coefficients are not sign-constrained.
Worked example
WeekMechanic active?Effect on cases
Promo weekYes+18% vs. baseline (gross lift)
Week afterNo, but promo_active_lag1=1−6% vs. baseline (pantry-loading give-back)
Net incremental effect of this mechanic is +12%, not +18% — see the ROI card in Phase 5 for how this gets carried through to the final number.
ECCBC example
Add promo_active_lag1 per mechanic as a control. A negative, credible coefficient means real forward-buying — the free-goods and multi-buy mechanics from the previous card are the most likely candidates to show this.

Phase 3 · Controls & Saturation

Shaping the response curve

Which controls to add

control_columns
Plain brief
Add temperature, a holiday flag, and a seasonality index as controls. Each covers a different gap the promo curves can't explain on their own.
ControlTypeWhat it covers
temperaturecontinuous, weekly avg per territoryYear-to-year weather anomalies a fixed seasonal cycle can't — a hotter-than-usual week lifts demand regardless of any promo
is_holidaybinary 0/1A coarse, simple flag for any non-Ramadan/Eid public holiday week — a cheaper alternative to a full Gaussian-basis event when the effect is a short, sharp one-week bump
seasonality_indexcontinuous, precomputed compositeA category-level consumption index (e.g. national soft-drink off-take trend) that captures market-wide seasonal drift the Fourier term alone may under-fit
Why these three specifically: each is a distinct, non-overlapping source of variation. Without them, the model has nowhere to put a hot week, a public holiday, or a category-wide trend — and it will incorrectly credit or blame a promo mechanic for that variation instead.
Note on is_holiday vs. Phase 2's calendar events: these are two different tools for two different kinds of holiday. Ramadan and Eid get the full EventEffect/GaussianBasis treatment from Phase 2 because their effect is smooth, multi-week, and moving. A plain civic holiday that's a single sharp week is usually fine as this simpler binary control — don't build a Gaussian-basis event for every minor holiday on the calendar.
ECCBC example
temperature per territory, is_holiday for Morocco's fixed civic holidays (New Year, Throne Day, Independence Day, etc.), and a seasonality_index built from ECCBC's own category-level volume trend if available, or a national beverage-consumption proxy if not.
Phase 3 of 6

How many Fourier terms for seasonality

yearly_seasonality=K
Plain brief
Start with a small number of Fourier terms. Add more only if the residual shows a seasonal pattern the model missed.
\[ s(t) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \Big[ a_k \sin(2\pi k t/P) + b_k \cos(2\pi k t/P) \Big] \]
Why: a low K gives a smooth, single-peak-per-year curve; a high K can fit sharp bumps — including ones that actually belong to Ramadan or a holiday, which is exactly the aliasing the calendar table and is_holiday control are meant to prevent.
ECCBC example
Start at K=2 for the temperature-driven summer/winter cycle; check residuals before raising it, since Ramadan's bump is already handled by its own event, not by Fourier.
Phase 3 of 6

Saturation family per mechanic

HillSaturation() / LogisticSaturation()
\[ f(x) = \frac{x^{s}}{k^{s}+x^{s}} \quad \text{(Hill)} \]
Options
HillSaturationLogisticSaturationMichaelisMentenSaturation
Why: Hill's extra shape parameter s lets the curve start flat before rising, which fits a discount that needs a minimum depth before shoppers react — logistic saturation always rises fastest at zero spend, which is often the wrong starting shape for a threshold-driven mechanic.
ECCBC example
A free-goods mechanic ("buy 10 get 1") plausibly has a real threshold — Hill's flat start models that; a simple price-off discount without a threshold may fit fine with logistic saturation instead.

Phase 4 · Fit & Select

Letting the data pick the functional form

Link function, worked concretely

link="identity" / "log"
Plain brief
Use the identity link when a promo adds a fixed number of cases everywhere. Use the log link when it adds a fixed percentage instead.
\[ \text{identity: } y = \mu + \varepsilon \qquad\qquad \text{log: } y = e^{\mu}\cdot(1+\varepsilon) \]
The concrete difference this makes
CellBaseline casesIdentity-link predictionLog-link prediction
Casablanca / Modern Trade (high-volume)10,000+500 cases (fixed add)+5% → +500 cases
Rabat / Traditional Trade (low-volume)800+500 cases (same fixed add — implausible here)+5% → +40 cases (scales with the cell)
Why this matters: the identity link forces the same absolute promo effect onto every cell regardless of size, which is usually wrong once your grain spans a 10x-plus volume range. The log link instead assumes the same percentage effect everywhere, which is the more realistic assumption when territory/channel/medaille/SKU cells differ hugely in scale — exactly ECCBC's situation with a full four-dim grain.
Decision rule: if the ratio between your largest and smallest modeled cell's baseline volume is small (roughly under 3-4x), identity is simpler and fine. If it's large — which a territory×channel×medaille×SKU grain almost always produces — start with log link as the leading candidate, and let cross-validation (next card) confirm it.
ECCBC example
With Casablanca Modern Trade flagship SKUs plausibly 10x+ a rural Traditional Trade cell's volume, put a log-link candidate first in the comparison set in Phase 4's candidate list, not as an afterthought.
Phase 4 of 6

How many candidate specs to fit

Plain brief
Fit two or three candidate specs. Do not fit every combination of every choice at once.
Why: the combinatorics of link × saturation × adstock explode fast; a focused set (e.g., log+Hill, log+Logistic, identity+Hill) covers the real open questions without burning a sampling budget on combinations nobody suspects.
ECCBC example
Three candidates for the first ECCBC pass: log-link/Hill, log-link/Logistic, identity-link/Hill — enough to test both open questions (link, curve shape) without a combinatorial sweep.
Phase 4 of 6

Cross-validation scheme

summary/cv.py, CRPS
Plain brief
Score each candidate on held-out future weeks. Pick the spec with the best out-of-fold score, not the best in-sample fit.
Why: in-sample fit always favors the most flexible spec; a time-sliced, out-of-fold score is what actually tells you which functional form generalizes to next quarter's data — the real use case for a TPO platform.
ECCBC example
Hold out the most recent 8-12 weeks of ECCBC data per fold — long enough to span at least one promo cycle per mechanic, so the held-out score reflects real promo-response accuracy, not just trend extrapolation.

Phase 5 · Calibrate & ROI

Turning a fitted curve into a trusted number

Use lift-test calibration?

lift_test.py
\[ \text{Potential} = \log\,\mathrm{Gamma}\big(\Delta_{\text{observed}} \mid \Delta_{\text{model}}(x)\big) \]
Plain brief
If ECCBC ran a controlled promo test, feed its result into the calibration step. If not, skip this decision for now.
Why: observational promo data alone can confound a mechanic's effect with whatever else was happening that week; a real controlled test anchors the saturation curve to ground truth the model can't get from history alone.
ECCBC example
If ECCBC has run even one geo-holdout or matched-market test for a mechanic, calibrate that mechanic's curve against it before trusting its ROI number for optimization.
Phase 5 of 6

Which incrementality reducer

IncrementalReducer subclasses
Why: the correct way to compute "incremental cases from this mechanic" depends on the link function chosen in Phase 4 — an identity-link model's increment is a simple subtraction; a log-link model's increment requires working back through the exponential, which is why the reducer is a link-specific class, not one formula.
ECCBC example
Whichever link won the Phase 4 cross-validation, use its matching reducer — mixing an identity-link reducer with a log-link fit will silently misstate every mechanic's ROI.
Phase 5 of 6

Gross ROI or net ROI

Plain brief
Subtract the lagged control's pull-back from the promo week's lift. Report that net number as ROI, not the raw gross lift.
Why: reporting gross lift alone overstates a mechanic that borrows heavily from the following week — two mechanics with identical gross lift can have very different true incrementality once forward-buying is netted out.
ECCBC example
Continuing Phase 2's worked example: +18% gross lift minus 6% pantry-loading give-back means net ROI is computed off +12%, not +18%.

Phase 6 · Optimize & Validate

Turning ROI into a plan

Budget scenario bounds

budget_bounds
Options
fixed — bounds pinned to current spendreduced — upper bound below currentincreased — upper bound above current
Why: the same optimizer call answers all three business questions — only the bound per cell changes, so the scenario is a configuration choice, not a different model or a different code path.
ECCBC example
A "reduce Casablanca Hypermarket 10%" ask becomes an upper bound of 0.9× that cell's current spend, passed straight into budget_bounds for that cell.
Phase 6 of 6

How far ahead to plan

num_periods
Plain brief
Set the planning horizon to match the real decision window. Do not default to a full year if the ask is one quarter.
Why: the optimizer solves jointly over budget and calendar within num_periods — a longer horizon than the actual decision window adds solve time and lets the optimizer plan around information the business won't act on anyway.
ECCBC example
For a next-quarter trade plan around Ramadan timing, set num_periods to the 13 weeks of that quarter, not 52.
Phase 6 of 6

Final sanity check

Plain brief
Compare the model's ROI ranking against the trade team's own view. Investigate any large disagreement before you trust the plan.
Why: a platform recommendation that contradicts the trade team's lived experience without an identifiable reason is more likely a data or specification issue than a genuine insight — this check is what earns the platform trust before it goes into production.
ECCBC example
If the model ranks a little-used mechanic as ECCBC's best performer, check whether that mechanic simply ran in too few, too-easy weeks (Phase 1's grain check) before presenting it as a recommendation.
